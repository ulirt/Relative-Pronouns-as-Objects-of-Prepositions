1 WHAT IS A RELATIVE PRONOUN?

A relative pronoun is a word that introduces a relative clause and connects it to a noun mentioned earlier (the antecedent). It replaces a repeated noun or pronoun and links two ideas into one sentence.

Vocabulary: Relative Pronouns

PronounSpanish equivalentUsed for
who 🔊/huː//huː/ - whoquien / quepeople (subject)
whom 🔊/huːm//huːm/ - whoma quien / al quepeople (object)
whose 🔊/huːz//huːz/ - whosecuyo / cuyapossession
which 🔊/wɪtʃ//wɪtʃ/ - whichel cual / queanimals, things, places
that 🔊/ðæt//ðæt/ - thatquepeople, animals, things (informal)

Example 1
The guide is very experienced. He knows the jungle well.
→ The guide who knows the jungle well is very experienced.
(El guía que conoce bien la selva es muy experimentado.)

Example 2
The plant treats fever. It grows near the river.
→ The plant which grows near the river treats fever.
(La planta que crece cerca del río trata la fiebre.)

Quick Practice — Let's put it into practice

1. The elder ______ teaches traditional medicine is very wise. (choose: who / which)

2. The forest ______ trees produce rubber is protected. (choose: whose / that)

2 WHAT IS A PREPOSITION? WHAT IS ITS OBJECT?

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another part of the sentence — usually location, direction, time, or association (e.g., in, on, at, with, about, from, for, under, beside, along).

A preposition is always followed by a noun or pronoun, called its object.

PrepositionTypical useExample
abouttopictalk about
withcompany / meanswork with, travel with
inplacelive in, work in
ondependence / surfacedepend on
fromorigin / sourcelearn from, buy from
forpurposecare for, look for
alongpathwalk along
besidepositionsit beside

We talked about the pink river dolphin.
ABOUT = preposition | the pink river dolphin = object of the preposition

The canoe traveled along the river.
ALONG = preposition | the river = object of the preposition

b) Grandmother learned the recipe from an old healer.

3 RELATIVE PRONOUNS AS OBJECTS OF PREPOSITIONS

In this topic, we are going to learn how a relative pronoun can replace the object of a preposition when we combine two sentences.

3.1 WHICH RELATIVE PRONOUNS DO WE USE?

The relative pronoun depends on what the repeated noun refers to:

Refers toFormal structureInformal structure
Peoplewhomwho / whom / that / Ø
Things, animals, placeswhichwhich / that / Ø

Ø means that the relative pronoun can be omitted.

Important:
Whose is not part of the main structure in this lesson because it expresses possession.

The main focus:
• People → whom (formal)
• Things/animals/places → which (formal)
In the informal structure, we can use who, whom, that, or Ø for people, and which, that, or Ø for things, animals, and places.

3.2 HOW DOES IT WORK?

Let's see an example with a person:

1 The biologist was an expert.
2 We talked about the biologist.
↓ The biologist is the object of the preposition about. We can replace it with WHOM.

The biologist about whom we talked was an expert.

The process:
1 Repeated noun: the biologist
2 Preposition: about
3 Object of the preposition: the biologist
4 Relative pronoun: whom

3.3 FORMAL STRUCTURE — PREPOSITION FIRST

In the formal structure, the preposition comes directly before the relative pronoun.

PEOPLE → WHOM
Structure:
Antecedent + preposition + whom + subject + verb

Example:
• The researcher with whom I worked was from Cobija.

THINGS, ANIMALS AND PLACES → WHICH
Structure:
Antecedent + preposition + which + subject + verb

Example:
• The forest in which we worked is in Pando.

PRACTICE — FORMAL STRUCTURE

1 Complete with whom or which.

a) The teacher _______ I worked was from Cobija.

b) The forest _______ we worked is protected.

2 Identify the parts.
The community with which we collaborated grows copoazú and cacao.

4 FORMAL STRUCTURE — PREPOSITION FIRST

In the formal structure, the preposition comes directly before the relative pronoun.

4.1 PEOPLE → WHOM

Structure:
Antecedent + preposition + whom + subject + verb

Example:
• The researcher with whom I worked was from Cobija.

More examples:
• The guide with whom we traveled was friendly.
• The teacher from whom I learned a lot is now retired.

4.2 THINGS, ANIMALS AND PLACES → WHICH

Structure:
Antecedent + preposition + which + subject + verb

The process:
1 Repeated noun: the forest
2 Preposition: in
3 Object of the preposition: the forest
4 Relative pronoun: which

Example:
• The forest in which we worked is in Pando.

More examples:
• The forest in which we worked is protected.
• The community with which we collaborated grows copoazú and cacao.
• The trail along which we walked leads to the Bella Flor lagoon.

EXERCISES — FORMAL STRUCTURE

A Complete with whom or which.

1. The teacher _______ I worked was from Cobija.

2. The forest _______ we worked is protected.

3. The community _______ we collaborated grows copoazú and cacao.

B Identify the parts.
The researcher with whom I worked was from Cobija.

5 INFORMAL STRUCTURE — PREPOSITION LAST

In the informal structure, the preposition stays at the end of the relative clause. The relative pronoun moves to the beginning, or it can sometimes be omitted.

It is very common in everyday conversation, spoken English and informal writing.

5.1 PEOPLE

Which relative pronouns do we use?
who / whom / that / Ø

Structure
Antecedent + who/whom/that/Ø + subject + verb + preposition

Examples
• The guide who I traveled with was friendly.
• The guide whom I traveled with was friendly.
• The guide that I traveled with was friendly.
• The guide I traveled with was friendly.
(Ø = the relative pronoun is omitted.)

Another example
The researcher who we worked with is from Cobija.
(El investigador con quien trabajamos es de Cobija.)

PRACTICE — PEOPLE (Informal)

1 Complete with who, whom, that or Ø.

a) The teacher _______ I spoke to is very kind.

b) The tourist _______ I talked to was from La Paz.

2 Rewrite the sentence using the informal structure.
The guide with whom I traveled was friendly.

5.2 THINGS, ANIMALS AND PLACES

Which relative pronouns do we use? which / that / Ø

Structure
Antecedent + which/that/Ø + subject + verb + preposition

Examples
• The lagoon which we swam in was full of fish.
• The lagoon that we swam in was full of fish.
• The lagoon we swam in was full of fish.

• The market that my mother buys fruit from sells Amazonian products.
(El mercado del que mi madre compra frutas vende productos amazónicos.)

PRACTICE — THINGS, ANIMALS AND PLACES (Informal)

1 Complete with which, that or Ø.

a) The forest _______ we worked in is protected.

b) The animal _______ we saw was a jaguar.

2 Rewrite the sentence using the informal structure.
The lagoon in which we swam was beautiful.

6 FORMAL VS. INFORMAL — QUICK COMPARISON

The two structures communicate the same idea. The main difference is the position of the preposition and the relative pronoun used.

Formal (preposition first)Informal (preposition last)
PeopleThe biologist about whom we talked...The biologist who we talked about...
Things / Animals / PlacesThe forest in which we worked...The forest which we worked in...

Remember:
• Formal: preposition + relative pronoun
• Informal: relative pronoun + subject + verb + preposition
